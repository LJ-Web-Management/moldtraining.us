Regulatory Guide

OSHA Mold Regulations: What Employers, Inspectors & Property Teams Need to Know

There is no OSHA mold standard, no OSHA mold testing requirement, and no OSHA mold certification. Here's what actually applies, where EPA and state rules fit in, and how training closes the gap.

See Our Courses Jump to FAQ

Short answer → OSHA has not set a mold-specific standard, exposure limit, testing requirement, or certification. Mold hazards are handled under the General Duty Clause plus existing standards (Respiratory Protection, PPE, Hazard Communication) when they apply to the work. The EPA publishes guidance, not certifications. A small number of states run their own licensing programs. Training that aligns with these principles helps employers document a reasonable, defensible safety program, even though it is not itself a government credential.

The Core Question

Is There an OSHA Mold Standard?

No. As of this writing, OSHA has not published a substance-specific standard for mold or a permissible exposure limit (PEL) for mold spores, unlike substances such as asbestos or lead that have dedicated standards. That doesn't mean mold is unregulated, it means it's handled through more general mechanisms:

General Duty Clause

Section 5(a)(1) of the OSH Act requires employers to provide a workplace free from recognized hazards likely to cause death or serious physical harm, which OSHA can apply to serious mold exposure cases.

Respiratory Protection

29 CFR 1910.134 applies when remediation work requires respirators, including fit testing, medical evaluation, and a written program.

PPE

29 CFR 1910.132 governs the gloves, eye protection, and protective clothing used during inspection and remediation work.

Hazard Communication

29 CFR 1910.1200 applies when cleaning agents or biocides used in remediation are hazardous chemicals requiring labeling and safety data sheets.

A Common Misconception

What About OSHA Mold Testing?

OSHA does not require air sampling, surface sampling, or any specific mold testing protocol, and has not set a numeric threshold for "how much mold is too much." Testing decisions in the field are typically driven by the visible extent of contamination, remediation scope, insurance or legal documentation needs, or a state/local requirement, not an OSHA mandate. Our Mold Inspector Certification Training covers when sampling is and isn't useful, aligned with IAC2 Mold Inspection Standards of Practice.

Where the EPA Fits In

Guidance, Not Certification

The EPA does not certify or license mold inspectors or remediation firms. It publishes non-regulatory guidance documents, most notably Mold Remediation in Schools and Commercial Buildings, that many training programs (including ours) align curriculum to. If a provider claims an "EPA certification," that claim should be verified directly with the EPA before you rely on it.

Beyond Federal Rules

Some States Go Further

A small number of states run their own mold assessor or remediation contractor licensing programs that go beyond anything OSHA or EPA requires federally. If you operate in one of those states, state licensing controls, not general awareness training.
